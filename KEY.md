# KEY — Bower planting and species relationships

## 1. Design thesis

A Bower should be a **managed multi-species plant community** supported by an architecturally deliberate lattice. It cannot be modelled as one vine texture laid over a static building. A reference UK composition is:

1. **Permanent woody architecture:** *Wisteria sinensis* **or** *Wisteria floribunda* 'Macrobotrys' dominates designated upper-shell routes, but remains prunable and mechanically decoupled where necessary.
2. **Evergreen continuity:** *Lonicera henryi* in suitable side zones; *Trachelospermum jasminoides* only in warm, sheltered sectors; restricted *Hedera helix* only where attachment, inspection and spread can be safely controlled.
3. **Summer flower and scent:** *Clematis viticella* 'Étoile Violette', *Clematis* 'Princess Diana', *Lonicera periclymenum* 'Graham Thomas', *Rosa* 'The Generous Gardener'. These mainly occupy lower-to-middle-height, independently serviceable supports.
4. **Winter and early spring flower:** *Clematis cirrhosa* 'Freckles' on a sheltered, sunny, accessible lower support; optionally *Clematis armandi* in mild sheltered sites.
5. **Autumn accent:** *Vitis coignetiae* in **one limited upper-sector** where its vigour and shade can be controlled.
6. **Annual colour and ground-level scent:** replace *Thunbergia alata* once a year in warm-season pockets, and place *Sarcococca confusa* beside shaded pedestrian approaches (the latter is not a climber).

**Do not plant every listed taxon in one Bower.** *Campsis × tagliabueana* 'Madame Galen', *Akebia quinata* and *Hydrangea anomala* subsp. *petiolaris* are site/geometry-specific alternatives. Plants occupying the same segment must pass combined light, root, pruning and maintenance-access checks.

## 2. Taxon / function / climbing-mechanism correlation

| Taxon | Function | Attachment mechanism | What Bower must provide | Default standing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *Wisteria sinensis* | Early-spring bloom, permanent woody scaffold | Woody stem twining; can be trained onto ties | Strong, continuous leader routes; strain-aware anchors; removable/adjustable ties and future access | Primary prototype; canopy alternative |
| *Wisteria floribunda* 'Macrobotrys' | Spectacular long pendant racemes | Woody stem twining | Support routes plus **free vertical hanging clearance** for racemes | Canopy alternative |
| *Lonicera henryi* | Winter-green foliage, summer flowers | Stem twining | Reachable narrow supports/cables, partial shade, trimming | Core evergreen candidate |
| *Trachelospermum jasminoides* | Evergreen foliage, scent in summer | Stem twining | Warm sheltered microclimate, fine-to-moderate climbing supports | Conditional evergreen |
| *Clematis cirrhosa* 'Freckles' | Late autumn–early spring flowers | Twining leaf petioles | Fine petiole-graspable support, sheltered sunny lower zone | Winter-flower module |
| *Clematis viticella* 'Étoile Violette' | Mid/late-summer flower | Twining leaf petioles | Fine support, annual hard-pruning access | Summer module |
| *Clematis* 'Princess Diana' | Bright mid-summer–early-autumn flower | Twining leaf petioles | Fine support, annual hard-pruning access | Summer colour alternative |
| *Rosa* 'The Generous Gardener' | Repeat pink flowers, scent | Scrambling cane; requires tying | Open frame, strong discreet tie points, thorn-safe visitor clearance | Low/mid human-scale module |
| *Lonicera periclymenum* 'Graham Thomas' | Summer scent, pollinator interest, berries | Stem twining | Guided support, sunlight to upper shoots / cooler root zone | Biodiversity/scent module |
| *Vitis coignetiae* | Dramatic red autumn foliage | Tendrils | Graspable lattice/wires, large spatial lane, thinning | Conditional autumn accent |
| *Hedera helix* | Evergreen coverage, autumn nectar, winter berries | Adventitious aerial-root attachment | Replaceable/inspectable textured support **not permitted to invade timber gaps** | Controlled option, often excluded |
| *Campsis × tagliabueana* 'Madame Galen' | Strong warm-site late-summer orange-red display | Aerial roots; supplemental training may be needed | Sunny sheltered, suitable textured/engineered surface and strong framework | Optional warm-site accent |
| *Clematis armandi* | Early-spring scent, evergreen leaves | Twining leaf petioles | Sunny sheltered site, petiole-sized trellis | Optional mild-site evergreen |
| *Hydrangea anomala* subsp. *petiolaris* | Shade-tolerant climbing, early-summer white flowers | Adventitious aerial-root attachment | Suitable dedicated contact surface, moisture and patience | Optional cool/shade zone |
| *Akebia quinata* | Semi-evergreen spring-flowering texture | Stem twining | Strong free-running route, restrained growth | Optional, site/invasiveness screening |
| *Thunbergia alata* | Annual bright summer colour | Herbaceous stem twining | Light low-level trellis and annual replant access | Replaceable annual |
| *Sarcococca confusa* | Ground-level evergreen and winter fragrance | **None — shrub** | Shaded soil bed near a path; no climbing surface | Ground companion |

*Taxonomic note:* Kew and RHS use the accepted spelling **Clematis armandi** (one final “i”), although **Clematis armandii** appears in some gardening contexts. The folder uses the authoritative spelling.

## 3. Relationships: complementary, competitive and operational

| Relationship | Intended effect | Main risk | Representation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wisteria + winter evergreen | Exposed winter woody scaffold still has local green zones | Large wisteria crown shades the evergreen in summer | Zonal placement; light competition; boundary pruning |
| Wisteria + summer Clematis | Spring and summer bloom without competing for the same flower calendar | Clematis gets buried under woody stems/leaves | Separate fine local trellis; avoid shared attachment segments |
| *L. henryi* + *C. cirrhosa* | Winter foliage plus scattered winter flowers | One overwhelms the other's limited lower growth zone | Adjacent **but distinct** climbing lanes |
| Honeysuckle + climbing rose | Summer scent, wildlife value and broad colour palette | Physical tangles and difficult maintenance | Separate tie points and trained shoot corridors |
| *Vitis* + all neighbouring climbers | Strong red autumn contrast | Large leaves and rapid vigour dominate light/canopy | Bounded optional sector, yearly thinning |
| Ivy + exposed timber | Hardy winter green; possible ecological value | Stem intrusion, leaf/wood moisture retention, inspection obstruction | Default **no direct timber attachment**; separate replaceable substrate |
| Wisteria + wisteria species | Different floral and twisting characteristics | Coupled woody growth and unmanageable mature mass | Choose one primary per structural lane unless specifically justified |
| Seasonal *Thunbergia* + perennials | Summer colour during permanent establishment | Competes with emerging perennial shoots if interwoven | Removable trellis, annual clearing and planting |

Use qualitative relationship categories first: `complementary`, `competes_for_light`, `competes_for_root_resources`, `attachment_conflict`, `maintenance_conflict`, `seasonal_relay`. Do **not** assign made-up interspecific coefficients before calibration.

## 4. Global structure: what depends on the entire Bower

The global structure model must provide the following **before** species are located:

- Full 3-D timber geometry, member IDs, structural exclusions and maintenance inspection corridors.
- A **support-path graph** representing accessible routes from each root station; measured path length is not the same as vertical height.
- Site coordinates, aspect, orientation, incident sunlight, sky view, shade from surrounding trees/buildings, prevailing wind exposure and local frost pockets.
- Rooting bed location, soil volume, pH, drainage, irrigation availability and estimated competition between planting stations.
- Visitor/program zones: entrances, occupied volumes, head clearance, hanging-flower clearance, seating, public reach zones and maintenance access.
- Project-specific visual goals: areas meant to remain visibly timber, autumn accent sectors, winter evergreen sectors, and where the viewer sees the flowering underside.
- Hazard and ecology constraints: toxic plant parts, thorn exposure, deadwood removal, invasive-species restrictions by region, protected wildlife and access for inspection.

**Height is not enough.** For an ideal **15 m diameter hemispherical shell**, radius is 7.5 m and the shortest base-to-apex meridional surface arc is **πR/2 ≈ 11.78 m**. Actual twining and training routes are longer or differently oriented. Published RHS mature-height ranges are not guaranteed path reach or yearly extension rates.

### Useful initial height/path classes (design heuristics, not botanical cut-offs)

| Surface-route requirement | Core tactic | Species families |
|---|---|---|
| <4 m | Close-range flowering, accessible seasonal management | Clematis, roses, star jasmine, *Thunbergia* |
| 4–8 m | Multilayer side canopy | Honeysuckle, star jasmine, wisteria where properly trained |
| 8–12 m | Primary vigorous woody routes plus lower-tier satellites | Wisteria, *Vitis*, selected *Campsis* |
| 12–20 m | One or more engineered/rooted climbing routes; evaluate intermediate planting platforms | Vigorous *Wisteria*, *Vitis*, climbing hydrangea on compatible surfaces |
| >20 m | Do not assume ground-rooted climbers fully cover the shell | Exposed timber, intermediate root levels, staged construction/planting experiment |

## 5. Required local structure: the plant-contact system

Bower's **manufactured lattice is a primary structural system**, but plant contact is often better handled by a **separate, replaceable, plant-specific secondary support network**:

- **For stem twiners:** rods, cables or rails with appropriately tested diameter, stable geometry and accessible lateral continuation; allow periodic repositioning of ties; do not encourage woody stems to constrict critical timber members.
- **For leaf-petiole climbers:** fine wires or slender trellis at close enough spacing that emerging leaves can contact them; validate diameter/contact empirically.
- **For tendrils:** fine-to-moderate graspable elements with nearby contact opportunities; tendril grasp range is species- and developmental-stage-specific.
- **For aerial-root climbers:** engineered textured or sacrificial panels/strips where attachment is allowed; direct colonisation of primary timber is **not automatically acceptable**.
- **For roses:** discrete tie points on open frames with access for cutting and replacement.
- **For hanging inflorescences:** collision-free vertical volume and clearance to visitor circulation. Especially important for long *W. floribunda* racemes.
- **For all species:** a documented clearance envelope for stem thickening, foliage, drainage, inspection, winter loads and pruning.

Never replace actual support-diameter studies with a global constant. Placeholders remain unresolved until measured.

## 6. Single UK reference palette / yearly spectacle

- **Jan–Feb:** evergreens and exposed woody lattice; *C. cirrhosa* and winter-scented *Sarcococca* at accessible edges.
- **Mar–Apr:** winter clematis tails off; optional *C. armandi* in mild sunny sectors; young leaves emerge.
- **May–Jun:** the major wisteria flowering peak, subject to plant maturity, spring frosts, bud survival and local conditions.
- **Jun–Aug:** roses, honeysuckle, summer clematis, star jasmine and optional warm-site *Campsis*.
- **Sep–Nov:** residual flowers; *Vitis* and deciduous wisteria autumn leaves; ivy flowers then berries; winter clematis begins where established.

**Design target, not biological fact:** test 25–40% of selected visually prominent areas with retained winter evergreen coverage, with exposed timber intentionally visible elsewhere. There is **no assurance** of continuous profuse winter flowers above 3–4 m; plan winter as foliage + scent + architectural skeleton + selected lower flowers.

## 7. Placement logic — qualitative order of operations

1. Filter species by **country/region legality and ecology**, climate, winter exposure and soil. Only then assess the visual palette.
2. Assign primary wisteria/root stations and long support routes; test raceme clearance and future woody-stem removal/inspection.
3. Reserve winter-evergreen zones with acceptable light and wind protection; keep roots and upper shoots supplied with light/water.
4. Add fine local climbing structures for lower seasonal clematis and compatible companions.
5. Add a small number of constrained seasonal accents (rose, vine, Campsis) rather than filling every gap.
6. Give each plant a **maintenance-access and pruning plan**; make infeasible plant/support combinations invalid.
7. Render monthly time slices, check winter appearance and repeat with the gardener model active.
