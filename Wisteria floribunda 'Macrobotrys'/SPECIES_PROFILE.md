# Wisteria floribunda 'Macrobotrys'

**Common name:** Long-racemed Japanese wisteria (RHS also lists Kyushaku/related synonyms)  
**Place in palette:** PRIMARY — alternate architectural wisteria species/cultivar

## Observational / horticultural baseline

| Trait | Currently supported description |
|---|---|
| Growth habit | Deciduous, woody, twining climber |
| Climbing or support mechanism | Stem twining; RHS describes Japanese wisteria as clockwise (viewpoint convention required). |
| RHS ultimate-height envelope | RHS: 8–12 m; 10–20 years to ultimate size class. |
| Hardiness | H6 (RHS). |
| Flower / leaf / fruit season | Strong late-spring/early-summer lilac-blue/violet flowers; pendulous racemes can reach 1 m or more; autumn yellow leaves. |
| Aspect and exposure | Full sun/partial shade; south/west sheltered sites, generous overhead clearance. |
| Soil | Moist but well-drained; tolerates common garden soil types. |

**Evidence discipline:** height classes and mature size times are qualitative UK horticultural descriptors, **not** yearly extension, support-path reach or physical force values. This file makes no claim to cultivar-calibrated numerical growth rates.

## Species-specific biological logic

Woody main axes with flowering spurs and long racemes; prior-season flower-bud initiation means the plant must retain appropriate buds and laterals through winter.

## What Bower must provide: local supports

Long, woody climbing routes with robust separated supports; leave vertical free space for racemes to hang without colliding with cross-members, lighting or visitors.

## Global structure placement logic

Upper shell/arch with visible underside; **prefer over *W. sinensis*** when very long suspended floral cascades are the essential design goal.

## Gardener intervention / pruning

Train and selectively thin; two-stage seasonal wisteria pruning, while protecting productive laterals. Inspect for attachment constriction.

## Interactions with the broader palette

Pair with evergreen side zones and lower summer flowers, but avoid shading racemes with nearby climbers or stacking aggressive vines.

## Risks, exclusions and scientific caveats

Long racemes need clear height; harmful if eaten; mature woody mass and wind response not validated. RHS lists synonym/status variants—preserve taxonomic aliases in final data.

## Parameters to obtain or calibrate

Raceme-length distribution and vertical density; species-specific coiling, extension, spur/bud response, age-to-flowering and canopy light transmission.

At minimum, bind all measured parameters to plant **age**, plant **part**, time step, site/climate, support properties and propagation/stock history. Keep missing values empty rather than extrapolating from species names or RHS maturity classes.
