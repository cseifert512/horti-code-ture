# Wisteria sinensis

**Common name:** Chinese wisteria  
**Place in palette:** PRIMARY — existing algorithm / alternative permanent canopy

## Observational / horticultural baseline

| Trait | Currently supported description |
|---|---|
| Growth habit | Deciduous, woody, long-lived climber |
| Climbing or support mechanism | Stem twining, **right-handed** helix: anticlockwise seen from above. The RHS says anticlockwise without stating a viewpoint; Valder (1995), Darwin and Flora of China agree on the geometry. The prototype twines this way. |
| RHS ultimate-height envelope | RHS: higher than 12 m; 10–20 years to RHS ultimate size category. This is not a calibrated extension rate. |
| Hardiness | H6 (RHS). |
| Flower / leaf / fruit season | Fragrant lilac/mauve flower racemes to ~30 cm; generally late spring, notably opening before foliage; yellowing leaves in autumn, bare woody framework in winter. |
| Aspect and exposure | Full sun or partial shade, preferably south/west and sheltered; full sun is important for profuse flowers. |
| Soil | Moist, well-drained; avoid prolonged waterlogging. |

**Evidence discipline:** height classes and mature size times are qualitative UK horticultural descriptors, **not** yearly extension, support-path reach or physical force values. This file makes no claim to cultivar-calibrated numerical growth rates.

## Species-specific biological logic

Long woody leaders and subordinate laterals/spurs; flower buds start developing in the preceding late summer. Bud survival is affected by drought, pruning and frost. Preserve bud cohort and stem lineage.

## What Bower must provide: local supports

Strong continuous route, gardener tie points and woody-stem clearance; leaders twine round or are tied along the glulam and its sub-lattice; track the places where thickening stems encircle a member.

## Global structure placement logic

Upper canopy/trunk lane with an uninterrupted viable root station and inspection access. Match seasonal bloom volume to visitor viewpoint; do not assume ground-rooted leaders can cross any large roof path.

## Gardener intervention / pruning

Gardener trains leaders during establishment; after canopy development, manage summer and winter spur pruning (typical guidance: Jul–Aug, Jan–Feb), trim unruly shoots and inspect ties.

## Interactions with the broader palette

Pairs with distinct winter evergreen and Group 3 clematis zones; severe shading will impair flowers below. Avoid treating *W. floribunda* as the same exact cultivar morphology.

## Risks, exclusions and scientific caveats

All parts potentially harmful if eaten; mature wood can become large and heavy; published height gives no safe loads or annual extension.

## Parameters to obtain or calibrate

Stem twining handedness in explicit frame; exploratory tip trajectories; yearly shoot-extension distribution; lateral/spur probabilities; flower-bud induction/survival; stem thickening; train/tie response; leaf area; wood mass.

At minimum, bind all measured parameters to plant **age**, plant **part**, time step, site/climate, support properties and propagation/stock history. Keep missing values empty rather than extrapolating from species names or RHS maturity classes.
