# Clematis 'Princess Diana'

**Common name:** Pink texensis-type clematis 'Princess Diana'  
**Accepted name:** RHS: *Clematis* 'Princess Diana' (T), Texensis Group (record 118677).  
**Place in palette:** OPTION — vivid pink summer accent

## Observational / horticultural baseline

| Trait | Currently supported description |
|---|---|
| Growth habit | Deciduous climber of herbaceous habit; Group 3 pruning. |
| Climbing or support mechanism | Twining leaf petioles. |
| RHS ultimate-height envelope | RHS: 2.5–4 m; 2–5 years to ultimate height class. |
| Hardiness | H5 (RHS). |
| Flower / leaf / fruit season | Bright reddish-pink tulip-shaped blooms from midsummer into early autumn. |
| Aspect and exposure | Full sun/partial shade; broadly adaptable aspects. |
| Soil | Moist but well-drained. |

**Evidence discipline:** height classes and mature size times are qualitative UK horticultural descriptors, **not** yearly extension, support-path reach or physical force values. This file makes no claim to cultivar-calibrated numerical growth rates.

## Species-specific biological logic

Flowers on current-year stems (Group 3) and tends to regenerate from the lower plant after late-winter pruning.

## What Bower must provide: local supports

Fine trellis/wire, separate lane from big woody wisteria arms; low enough for annual pruning and retraining.

## Global structure placement logic

Visitor-level colour as an alternative or companion to *Étoile Violette*; use discrete colour fields rather than chaotic co-mingling.

## Gardener intervention / pruning

Hard prune in late winter/early spring under Group 3 guidance, then direct new growth.

## Interactions with the broader palette

Affects summer colour rather than structural woody architecture; competition with other clematis and honeysuckle requires management.

## Risks, exclusions and scientific caveats

Do not model as a self-twining wisteria. Skin irritant; harmful to pets (rabbits) if eaten (RHS).

## Parameters to obtain or calibrate

Bud/flower timing on new stems; colour and flowering density; petiole attachment success; seasonal shoot extension after pruning.

At minimum, bind all measured parameters to plant **age**, plant **part**, time step, site/climate, support properties and propagation/stock history. Keep missing values empty rather than extrapolating from species names or RHS maturity classes.
