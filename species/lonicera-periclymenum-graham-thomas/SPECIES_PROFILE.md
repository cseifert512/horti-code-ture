# Lonicera periclymenum 'Graham Thomas'

**Common name:** Common honeysuckle cultivar 'Graham Thomas'  
**Place in palette:** CORE OPTION — biodiversity and fragrant summer flowering

## Observational / horticultural baseline

| Trait | Currently supported description |
|---|---|
| Growth habit | Deciduous twining woody climber. |
| Climbing or support mechanism | Stem twining. |
| RHS ultimate-height envelope | RHS: 4–8 m; 5–10 years to ultimate size category. |
| Hardiness | H6 (RHS). |
| Flower / leaf / fruit season | Fragrant white flowers ageing cream-yellow over a long summer flowering period; red ornamental berries. |
| Aspect and exposure | Full sun/partial shade; aspect flexible, exposure tolerated; partial shade often favourable. |
| Soil | Fertile, moist and well-drained. |

**Evidence discipline:** height classes and mature size times are qualitative UK horticultural descriptors, **not** yearly extension, support-path reach or physical force values. This file makes no claim to cultivar-calibrated numerical growth rates.

## Species-specific biological logic

Summer flower development on actively growing climber; stem twining and winter deciduous state. Preserve berries as optional wildlife/public-safety output.

## What Bower must provide: local supports

Continuous twining support with access for cutting vigorous runners and monitoring berry position.

## Global structure placement logic

Perimeter/lower-to-middle lane with flowers close enough to visitors to smell, plus ecological benefit for pollinators.

## Gardener intervention / pruning

Prune/control after flowering as needed and maintain distinct lane from evergreen honeysuckle.

## Interactions with the broader palette

Adds later fragrant season to wisteria; do not conflate native species ecology with non-native commercial cultivars in every jurisdiction.

## Risks, exclusions and scientific caveats

Berry is ornamental and not food; can entangle neighbours.

## Parameters to obtain or calibrate

Flowering window by site, branching, wildlife flower-use observations, twining geometry, winter leaf loss, pruning response.

At minimum, bind all measured parameters to plant **age**, plant **part**, time step, site/climate, support properties and propagation/stock history. Keep missing values empty rather than extrapolating from species names or RHS maturity classes.
