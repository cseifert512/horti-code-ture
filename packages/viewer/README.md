# @horti/viewer

Renders a Bower from `@horti/core`, and the plants that grow on it, at the visual quality of the `Wisteria/` prototype. It reads core data and calls core functions; it holds no growth logic and adds no dates of its own.

```bash
npm run viewer:dev                      # http://localhost:5193
npm run viewer:build                    # static build in packages/viewer/dist
npm run portrait -- wisteria-sinensis   # stills of one taxon on the specimen frame, and a contact sheet, in out/portraits/
```

Whoever gives a taxon its look works from [`TAXON_LOOKS.md`](TAXON_LOOKS.md).

## What it shows

- **The structure.** Glulam ribs and the timber lattice as swept laminated sections, steel cables as fine tubes at their true diameter, stone pads under the feet, a planting pit at each root station, a lawn, a distant belt of trees, sky and soft shadows. Glulam shows its laminations, glue lines, finger joints and, at a cut end, end grain; it is weathered a little, as a look and not as a claim. Switch between the vault segment, the 15 m hemisphere and a design of your own.
- **The sun, where it really is.** The sun is placed by the core (`sunAt`) for the site the Bower stands on, the date shown and an hour of the day (Greenwich Mean Time; the Scene controls have the hour and, when no plants are shown, the date). The site is the one a description declares, or southern England, assumed and said to be. The two sliders remain as an override ("Set by hand"). Walls, buildings and trees a site declares are drawn as plain massing and cast shadow, because the light model shades by them.
- **Light on the structure** (key `L`). Every stretch of route and every root station in the colour of the light the core says it has (`exposureField`): hours of direct sun on a clear day, or light as a share of an open site's, for the month of the date shown, any month, or the year. With plants on show it is read with the leaf of the year shown. One ramp, dark for least to pale yellow for most, over the whole range the measure can take; a switch spreads it over the structure's own range to see small differences. The panel gives the figure for each side of the structure and, from the run's report, each plant against what its profile accepts.
- **Stills.** "Save this view" draws the picture at 4K, 5K, 8K or the window's size, in tiles at twice the size in each direction, and saves a PNG. "Every viewpoint" does the same from each place the scenario's visitors stand, on the date shown: go to the date first.
- **A design read from files.** "A design..." takes a Bower description (`*.bower.json`) and, with it, a planting plan (`*.planting.json`): choose the two together, or drop them on the window. The arbour of `examples/` is there as a ready choice. Which file is which is told from what is inside, not from its name; a description alone opens as the structure with nothing planted. The core reads and checks both (`readBower`, `parsePlantingPlan`, `plantingOn`). Every member and cable of the description is drawn, rectangular or round, straight or kinked, and the camera is set to the size of the thing: the arbour is 4 m across, not 15. What the core warns of (a cable that touches no timber, route that no root station can reach) is listed under the name of the design. A file that is refused shows the core's reason in full, with the name of the file, and the picture stays as it was. The plan is grown trained or untended for the number of years you set; its report is drawn beside it, with the winter cover only if the description names where a visitor stands.
- **The design overlay** (key `O`). Pick a root station and a route budget in metres. Every plant route is coloured by its shortest route length from that station and greyed out beyond the budget, part-way along a route where the budget runs out there. The panel gives nodes reached, route within budget, highest height reached, and the route length to the highest node, with a white-cased line along that route. Two buttons set the budget to the height of the highest node and to the route length to it, which is the point: on the hemisphere 7.5 m does not reach the 7.5 m crown and 11.78 m does. Below that: metres of glulam, of timber sub-lattice and of steel cable, and steel's share of plant route. Hover over a route for its id, kind, grasp diameter and length; click to keep it.
- **Plants.** Any `RenderData` (`packages/core/src/render/contract.ts`, contract 2): stems, leaves, flowers, fruit, and what a plant holds on with (tendrils, coiled leaf stalks, clinging rootlets, prickles), each drawn with the look of its own taxon. The engine's reference scenarios run in the browser from the files in `species/`: wisteria on the vault segment or the 15 m hemisphere; the mixed planting on the vault; and the KEY.md reference palette on the hemisphere, twelve plants, trained, untended or with ivy. A scenario is drawn on the Bower it was grown on, so the mixed planting brings its five-station vault and the palette its twelve-station dome. There is also a hand-made render test that works on either Bower: one plant in the wisteria's models with hanging inflorescences and pods, and four more that carry each kind of hold and flowers held in a direction.
- **The design report.** Beside any engine scenario: the core's flowering calendar (plants by months, brighter the more flowers were open on the 15th, hatched where a plant's flowering is not modelled, with a line for the months when nothing flowers) and winter evergreen cover against the 25 to 40% design target, read two ways: as laid out (the share of route held by plants that keep their leaves) and as seen from each of the scenario's viewpoints, with how much of the timber the leaf hides. Selecting a month goes to its date; selecting a viewpoint stands the camera there.
- **Time.** A timeline scrubs or plays through the run (space bar). The gardener's log is listed beside the picture: entries not yet made are dimmed, the latest is marked, and clicking one goes to its day. The data's `statement` and the count of assumptions behind the run stay on screen with the picture, including when the interface is hidden (key `H`). `H` and `O` work wherever the focus is, except where something is being typed.

- **A specimen.** `?specimen=<taxonId>` grows one plant of that taxon on the specimen frame (`examples/specimen-frame.bower.json`: a glulam post, a trellis of 40 mm laths and three 5 mm cables, so that every way of climbing finds a hold at the foot) and shows it on a date, whole or close on one real organ of the run. `years=<n>` (default 6), `date=<YYYY-MM-DD>` (default: the day the viewer would open on), `view=whole|leaf|flower|fruit|stem|hold`, `care=untended` (default: trained by a gardener), `bare=1` hides the interface, `still=1` stops after the final frame. The organ looked at is chosen from the render data: for `flower`, an inflorescence the core says is open on that date. Where there is none, the page says so across the top, with the days on which there is one, and shows the whole plant. A taxon that cannot be grown is refused in words. A specimen is drawn at full detail and without wind, and the page sets `window.__ready` (and `data-ready` on `<body>`) to `true` once the frame shown is final, or to `'error'`; until then a dark curtain covers the picture.

URL parameters: `bower=vault|hemisphere|arbour|yard` (the last two open the example design, and the same arbour in a walled yard with a declared site), `hour=<0-24>` and `date=<ISO>` (the sun), `sun=hand`, `light=1` with `measure=share` and `stretch=1`, `quality=draft|high`, `years=<n>` (how long a design is grown, 1 to 30), `overlay=1`, `station=<id>`, `budget=<m>`, `plants=trained|untended|mixed-trained|mixed-untended|palette-trained|palette-untended|palette-ivy|fixture`, `day=<n>`, `ui=0`, `wind=<0 to 2>` (there from the first frame, so `wind=0` gives the same still every time), `cam=x,y,z,tx,ty,tz` (three.js frame), and for a specimen `specimen`, `date`, `view`, `care`, `bare`, `still`.

## Portraits

`npm run portrait -- <taxonId> [--years 6] [--care trained|untended] [--out out/portraits] [--port 5197] [--only whole,leaf,flower,fruit,stem,hold] [--size 1600x1000] [--jobs 3]` (`scripts/portrait.ts`, no dependencies of its own) grows the specimen in Node to find its dates, starts the viewer on the port if it is not already there (and leaves it running: `--stop-server` stops it), and takes each still with headless Edge, or Chrome where there is no Edge:

```
msedge --headless=new --screenshot=<file> --window-size=1600,1000 --virtual-time-budget=15000 "http://localhost:5197/?specimen=<id>&years=6&date=<date>&view=<view>&bare=1&still=1"
```

It writes `<out>/<taxonId>/<view>-<date>.png`: the whole plant on a day of each of four seasons of the last year, then a leaf, a flower, a fruit, a stem and a hold, each on a day when the core's data says it is there (`src/specimen/specimen.ts`, `portraitDates`; the reason for each date is printed). A view the run has nothing for is skipped, with the reason. `<out>/index.html` is a contact sheet of every taxon found there. Edge's launcher returns before the picture is written, so the script waits for the file; it then reads the picture back and reports a still that shows the viewer's curtain, or one flat colour, as failed. WebGL draws in headless Edge here with no further flags.

| `src/plant/look.ts` | What a look can say: `TaxonLook`, `LeafAsset`, `FlowerAsset`, `FruitAsset`, `AppendageAsset`, `WoodLook`, and the triangle budgets. |

## Things to know

- **Two ways of drawing a frame.** The quick way is the default for anything that moves (the timeline, the camera, leaves in the wind): the scene straight to the window, tone-mapped as it is drawn, as the viewer always did. The careful way (`scene/pipeline.ts`) is for pictures that are looked at: the scene is drawn in linear light into a multisampled target with its depth kept; the shading of corners and crevices (ambient occlusion, a reach of 0.4 m in the world that draws in when seen close) is worked out from that depth and blurred along surfaces; exposure, the Khronos Neutral tone curve and the sRGB encoding are applied once, at the end; overlays are drawn after that in their own exact colours. Both use the same curve and exposure, so the two pictures differ only in that shading, the finer edges and the sky's cloud. The careful way is used for every exported still, for a specimen's portrait, for a scene that has come to rest with nothing moving in it (no plants, or no wind), and always with `?quality=high`. `?quality=draft` never uses it.
- **What that costs.** Measured in a headless Edge on Intel UHD integrated graphics at 1600 x 900, `main` (b53eaa5) and this viewer back to back, three runs each. Vault with the mixed planting: a frame at rest 23 to 28 ms on `main` and 24 to 27 ms here; playing the timeline 39 to 43 frames a second on `main` and 36 to 39 here. Hemisphere with the palette: at rest 17 to 18 ms on `main` and 17 to 20 ms here; playing 43 to 48 frames a second and 42 to 45. As means: at rest 1% and 7% slower, playing 6% and 2% slower. A frame drawn the careful way takes 38 to 44 ms on the vault and about 34 ms on the hemisphere. A 4K still takes two to three and a half seconds. Timings move by a tenth or more between runs on that machine. Not measured: a discrete GPU, a high-density screen, a phone.
- **The sky's cloud is in the careful pictures only.** It is worked out at every pixel of sky in every frame, which cost about 2 ms a frame on the vault. So the sky is clear on screen at default quality, and has its high thin cloud in stills, portraits and `?quality=high`. A still saved from a default-quality view therefore has cloud the screen did not show.
- **How plants take light is shared by every taxon** (`plant/shaders.ts`). A leaf or a petal is lit on whichever face the sun is on and shows the sun's light through it on the other, by the look's `translucency`; both take the sun's shadow from whatever stands in its way, and neither from the leaf itself. Gloss is the look's `roughness`. A stem is shaded where it lies against its support. Nothing in `look.ts`, `kit.ts` or a taxon module was changed for this. It hangs on two lines of three.js's own shaders; a test says so if a release moves them, and the old unshadowed glow is used in that case.
- **The sky is fair weather.** A little high cloud, always. There is no overcast day and no weather: the hours of sun the light overlay shows are those a clear day allows.
- **The horizon and the lawn are dressing.** The belt of trees is a painted silhouette a few hundred metres off; it says nothing about a site. So are the weathering of the timber and the stone edge to the walk.
- **A bare deciduous tree casts no shadow in the picture.** The light model lets about half the sun through a bare crown; the picture cannot draw half a shadow, so it draws the crown thin and leaves the shadow out.

- **Section orientation is inferred.** The core gives a member's centre line and section size but not which way the section is turned. `frames.ts` takes the outer face to be where the member's plant routes lie, borrows from the lattice where a rib carries no route, and falls back to the plane of the arch. A member read from a design file has its routes on its centre line, so there is nothing to infer from: its section is stood upright, with one face up, which is the rule the core uses to lay a tied stem on the upper face (`sim/stem-path.ts`). A description cannot yet say that a section is turned any other way. Each member reports how it was oriented.
- **A stray element moves the camera.** The view is set to everything in the description. A cable drawn far from the rest pulls the camera back to take it in, which is also how it gets noticed.
- **Looks are per taxon.** Mechanism traits do not transfer, and neither do looks. Eleven taxa have leaf models of their own and ten have flower models: wisteria (the prototype's raceme, pinnate leaf and pod), two honeysuckles, three clematis, climbing rose, climbing hydrangea, star jasmine, vine (leaf only) and ivy (two leaf forms, umbels, berries and clinging rootlets). The other six (*Wisteria floribunda* 'Macrobotrys', *Clematis* 'Princess Diana', the trumpet vine, akebia, thunbergia and the sweet box) have a module that draws the stand-in, a plain leaf and no flowers, and the caption says their look is provisional. What a run has and a look cannot draw (flowers, fruit, a kind of hold, a named form) is left out and named in the caption.
- **The models other than the wisteria's are simple, and were made from descriptions, not from specimens.** Shapes and colours follow the taxon profiles and the engine session's notes. The wood of each, and the first plain tendril, coiled stalk, rootlets and prickle, are first values. They need a horticulturist's eye before they are shown as likenesses.
- **Each taxon has its own wood.** The stems of each taxon are a mesh of their own, in a bark material that takes its colours, its ages (how soon a shoot turns woody, when bark furrows) and its grain from that taxon's `WoodLook`. The geometry of a stem, and its thickness and age, are the same for all and come from the data.
- **A flower or fruit is held as the data says.** With no `direction` in the data it is drawn as its model was authored, hanging or standing plumb, turned to a compass bearing of its own; with one, its axis is turned on to it.
- **Holds are drawn only where the engine places them.** It places a tendril, a coiled stalk, rootlets or a prickle only where the taxon's growth model gives their spacing and length. Where those are unknown a run has none, and the `hold` view of a specimen shows a stem lying on its support instead.
- **Two things are added for the picture,** and both are written in `adapter.ts`: within the time the core says an inflorescence is open, opening and then petal fall run up it from the base; and a new stem point is eased in over its first day so stems lengthen smoothly between whole days.
- **Not in the contract, so not drawn from engine data:** ground litter (the shader program is ported and unused), flower colour per taxon, and how much room an inflorescence has to swing.
